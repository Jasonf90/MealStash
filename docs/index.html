Your family's recipes, kept properly.

MealStash is a recipe app for iPhone and iPad that behaves like a kitchen notebook rather than a website: no forty-paragraph preamble, no autoplaying video, no account to create, and nothing about you sent anywhere.

What it does

Rescues handwritten cards

Photograph a stained index card and MealStash reads it on the device, splitting it into a title, ingredients and steps. The photo never leaves your phone.

Cooks with you

Cooking Mode shows one step at a time in type you can read from across the counter, with only that step's ingredients in view. Tap a duration to start a timer; it keeps running on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island so you can put the phone down.

Plans the week and writes the list

Fill a weekly planner, then turn it into a grocery list grouped by aisle, with duplicate ingredients combined and pantry staples filtered out. Send it to Apple Reminders so it is on your watch at the shop.

Shares with the household

A Family Circle syncs recipes between nearby devices directly, over your local network. No account, no server, no subscription for the people you share with.

Prints like a cookbook

Any recipe prints as a 4×6 card, and your whole library exports as a press-ready Family Cookbook PDF. Every printed card carries a QR code that imports it straight back into the app.

Privacy is the architecture, not a promise

MealStash has no backend. There is no server to send your recipes to, which is why there is no account to create and nothing to opt out of. Sync between your own devices runs through your own iCloud account; family sync runs device to device over your local network.

No analytics. No advertising. No third-party code of any kind. Read the full privacy policy — it is short, because there is little to say.
